## Vision Statement
In this project aims to advance current understandings of play and its association with child development through the lens of Behavioural Genetics. We aim to understand the relative contributions of genetics and environment in shaping the how play develops and contributes to early childhood development. 


## What is the project about?
It is widely agreed that play is crucial to development. However, questions about how play develops and whether play uniquely contributes to child development is less understood. Although behavourial genetics methodologies have been widely used to address ateiological questions about varying aspects of development; it has yet to be applied to the study of play. This project will use variables related to children, their parents, and the genetic propensities of both and use these variables to explore linkages or interactions between home environments, play, and developmental outcomes for children. Existing cohort datasets from three UK longitudinal studies (TEDS and ALSPAC) will be used in a tiered approached to test hypotheses and attempt to replicate findings. The goal is to predict individual differences in children’s play, the interaction between them with the goal of learning whether associations between play and development are mediated by shared genetic or environmental factors. This work is important for clarifying the nature and origin of play to the play research community, play practitioners, and policy makers.
